WHAT WILL BE ASSESSED
The three questions come from this first stretch, where the hosts argue about whether the
Myers-Briggs measures anything real. Focus on three things. First, the Forer effect (also called
the Barnum effect): the 1949 experiment by psychologist Bertram Forer, in which he gave every
student the SAME personality profile -- one he had lifted from an astrology magazine -- and they
rated it 4.3 out of 5 for accuracy; plus the finding that people rate a test as more valid when
its description is favorable. Second, the reliability problem: the Myers-Briggs claims your type
is innate and never changes, yet somewhere between 39 and 76 percent of people get a different
result when they retake it just five weeks later, and only the introversion-extraversion scale
tracks with other personality instruments. Third, the contrast with the Big Five, which was
developed and independently validated by many teams over decades, versus the Myers-Briggs, which
is a self-report popularization of one of Jung's theories. Note also the honest opening: both
hosts say the framework can still be a constructive way to talk about differences. You do not
need the second half of the episode, the history of Katharine and Isabel Briggs.
