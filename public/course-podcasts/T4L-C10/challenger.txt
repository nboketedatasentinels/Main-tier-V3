Birhane covers a lot in this conversation, so the three questions draw only from her
points on the limits of modelling, not the middle stretch about who gets hired in
computing. Focus on three claims. First, that human behaviour is a complex adaptive
system she calls non-compressible, so a model can at best catch a snapshot of a moving
target. Second, that the belief that huge amounts of data will eventually give you
accuracy is what she calls a huge fallacy, and how shiny models revive already debunked
ideas like reading emotion or gender off a face. Third, her argument that some things,
like friction, should never be turned into a problem to be solved. You can skip the
parts on academic hiring and the closing funding credits.
