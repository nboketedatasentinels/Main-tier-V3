The Real Org Chart

Map the three power types operating in your team -- hard, soft, and expert. Then name the three biggest divergences between the real chart and the formal one. Each divergence comes with a cost -- the specific way it is showing up in the AI rollout. The formal chart is the one HR has on file. The real chart is the one the team operates by. Most AI rollouts run aground on the gap between the two.

What good looks like
Aim for these as you go.
  • Each power type is mapped with named people, not abstract roles. Initials are fine -- positions are not.
  • The three divergences are the genuinely biggest ones, including any where you yourself appear lower or higher than the formal chart says.
  • Each divergence cost is tied to a specific incident in the AI rollout -- a meeting derailed, a decision routed around the formal owner, a sign-off that did not happen.
  • The map names at least one divergence where expert authority sits below the formal authority -- because that is where AI rollouts most often fracture.

Step 2
Map the Three Power Types

For each power type, name the people who actually hold it -- by initial or short reference. Hard power is positional and budgetary. Soft power is who the room defers to socially. Expert power is whose technical judgement settles the disagreement. The three rarely line up. Where they do not, the AI rollout is most exposed.

Step 3
Name the Three Biggest Divergences -- and What Each Is Costing

Look at the three power maps. Where does the real chart diverge most sharply from the formal one? Pick the three biggest divergences -- the gaps that have the highest real-world voltage. For each one, name the divergence concretely (who has more or less power than the formal chart says) and the specific cost the AI rollout is paying for it. Not "communication friction". A specific decision routed around someone, a specific sign-off that did not happen, a specific meeting where the formal owner was not in the room when the call was made.

AI-Powered Activity

Use AI to Pressure-Test the Map

Real org chart maps fail at the same two places every time -- the practitioner picks the safest divergences (the ones that are about other people, not themselves), and the costs collapse into "communication friction" once stripped of specifics. AI as an outside reader catches both. Use it before you submit.

1
Test Whether You Picked the Genuinely Biggest Divergences
Ask AI to test whether the three divergences you named are the genuinely biggest ones -- including any where you yourself sit higher or lower than the formal chart says.
Paste this prompt
I am mapping the real org chart of my team during an AI rollout. The three power types I have mapped are: Hard: [paste]. Soft: [paste]. Expert: [paste]. The three biggest divergences I have named are: [paste each one with its cost]. Pressure-test this. Specifically: (1) Have I picked the genuinely biggest divergences, or the safest ones to name -- the ones that are about other people, not me? (2) Is there a divergence I have not named where I myself appear higher or lower on the real chart than the formal one? (3) Looking at the power maps, is there a divergence between hard and expert power that the AI rollout is sitting on top of, that I have not named because it is uncomfortable to name?
What did AI flag? What divergence did you miss -- and is it about you?
2
Stress-Test the Cost Statements
Ask AI to test whether each cost is tied to a specific AI rollout incident -- or whether it has slipped into "communication friction" or "team alignment" generic.
Paste this prompt
My three divergence costs are: [paste each cost]. Pressure-test on three criteria. (1) Is each cost tied to a specific incident in the AI rollout -- a meeting derailed, a decision routed around the formal owner, a sign-off that did not happen -- or has it slipped into "communication friction" or "team alignment" generic? (2) Could a different practitioner reading these costs tell what is at stake in my AI rollout specifically, or could they apply to any team? (3) For the cost that is most generic, rewrite it with operational specificity -- the date, the meeting, the named person, the consequence.
Which cost was most generic? What is the tightened version?
AI Lens
Six months from now, when the AI rollout has shifted -- new tooling, new role definitions, new budget pressure -- which of the three power types is most likely to redistribute, and which divergence is most likely to widen rather than close? What would tell you the redistribution had started before the org chart catches up?
Take This to Peer-to-Peer

Before you submit, talk it through.

Real org chart maps fall apart at the same place every time -- the divergences are about other people, not the practitioner, and the costs are written in language that lets the formal chart off the hook. A peer who has done their own map can hear both faults at the same speed an assessor will.

What to bring

Read the three power types aloud, then the three divergences. Ask your peer one question: of the three divergences, which one was uncomfortable to write? If they cannot tell -- and you wrote three that were comfortable -- the map is not yet honest. The honest map has at least one divergence the practitioner did not want to put on the page.

Three questions for your peer to ask you
  • Of the three divergences, is at least one about you -- where you sit higher or lower on the real chart than the formal one says?
  • Each cost -- is it tied to a specific incident in the AI rollout, or has it slipped into "communication friction" or "team alignment" generic?
  • Where hard power and expert power diverge -- is that divergence on the page, or did you skip it because it is the uncomfortable one?
