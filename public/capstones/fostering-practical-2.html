What good looks like
Aim for these as you go.
  • The Blueprint is one page -- five sections, sharp, deployable. Not aspirational language stretched into an essay.
  • The lowest pillar from Week 1 is carried forward by name, including the cost statement that named what it has been costing the AI-era work.
  • The alternative behaviour is a specific move you will run, not "be more present" or "listen better".
  • The three recurring moments are real, repeating events in the next 30 days -- not "team meetings in general".
  • The 30-day metric is observable. Someone other than you could tell whether it had been met.

AI-Powered Activity

Use AI to Pressure-Test the Blueprint

Blueprints fail at the same two places every time -- the alternative behaviour collapses into "be more present" once put under pressure, and the 30-day metric ends up unobservable when stripped of feeling-language. AI as an outside reader catches both faster than a peer can. Use it before you submit.

1
Test the Alternative Behaviour
Ask AI to test whether the alternative is a specific behaviour or whether it has slipped into a slogan -- "be more present", "listen harder", "show up for the team".
Paste this prompt
I am writing a Heart-Centered Blueprint for my team. The lowest pillar I am addressing is: [paste your pillar]. The alternative behaviour I have designed is: [paste your alternative behaviour]. Pressure-test it. Specifically: (1) If I asked a stranger to watch me in a meeting, would they be able to tell I was running the alternative behaviour, or is it observable only to me? (2) Has the alternative collapsed into a slogan -- "be more present", "listen better", "show up for the team" -- once stripped of detail? (3) What is the version of the alternative behaviour that someone could imitate by watching me do it once? Rewrite the weakest part with operational specificity.
What did AI flag? What is the tightened alternative behaviour?
2
Stress-Test the 30-Day Metric
Ask AI to test whether the metric is genuinely observable -- whether someone other than you could confirm it -- or whether it has slipped into "feel" language that cannot be checked.
Paste this prompt
My 30-day metric for the Blueprint is: [paste your metric]. Pressure-test on three criteria. (1) Could someone other than me -- a peer, a partner, an assessor -- look at observable evidence in 30 days and tell whether the metric had been met? (2) Does the metric depend on subjective feelings ("feels heard", "is more engaged", "morale lifted") or on observable artefacts (the documented record, attendance, a behaviour change)? (3) If the metric is met, does that prove the alternative behaviour landed -- or could it be met without the underlying behaviour shifting? Rewrite the weakest part to be observable and unambiguous.
Where did the metric go subjective? What is the observable version?

Take This to Peer-to-Peer

Before you submit, talk it through.

Blueprints fall apart at the same place every time -- the alternative behaviour sounds operational on the page, but a peer reading it cannot tell what they would see if they watched you run it. If your peer cannot describe the behaviour back to you in their own words, the Blueprint is not yet deployable.

What to bring

Read the Blueprint aloud, in order. Then ask your peer to tell you, without looking at the page, what they would see if they watched you run the alternative behaviour in the next incident review. If they can describe a specific scene -- the order of who speaks, what gets opened on the screen, what gets written down -- the Blueprint is deployable. If they say "you would be more empathetic", the Blueprint is still aspirational.

Three questions for your peer to ask you
  • The alternative behaviour -- could you describe it to me as a specific scene, or only as a quality I would bring to the room?
  • The three recurring moments -- are they real events in the next 30 days, with dates, or are they "team meetings in general"?
  • The 30-day metric -- if I came back in 30 days, could I check whether it had been met without asking you how you feel about it?
