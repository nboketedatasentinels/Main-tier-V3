Capstone Part B · Marked Submission
This Practical is also the Week 6 Capstone artefact. Your assessor marks it against the Capstone rubric -- it carries 25% of your final grade as the second half of the Combined Capstone. The same submission, marked once, counts for both the Practicals Portfolio and the Capstone.
What good looks like
Aim for these as you go.
  • The plan is six sections -- each one short, specific, deployable on Monday. Not a 12-page document trying to demonstrate effort.
  • Each section names a specific cadence, decision, signal, intervention, development move, or protocol -- with dates or frequencies attached.
  • The Lessons Synthesis is in your own voice -- not the language of the course. What you actually now know that you did not know in Week 1.
  • The Synthesis names at least one belief you came in with that turned out to be wrong, and what replaced it.
  • Each section ties back to one of the previous five Practicals -- the Synthesis shows that the journey worked, not that you read the materials well.

Step 2
Design the Six-Section Operating Plan

Six sections. Each one short. The plan reads as a single deployable artefact -- a page a peer could pick up in 90 seconds and tell you what you are running, on what cadence, with what signals, and what triggers an intervention. If a section grows past five lines, it is doing too much. Cut, do not pad.

THE 90-DAY PLAN
90-Day Operating Plan
Six sections · one page · deployable Monday morning of Week 7
01
Meeting Cadence
The recurring meetings that hold the operating system together for 90 days. Frequency, length, named purpose. Not the meetings that already exist out of habit -- the ones that are doing real work.
02
Decision Rights
Where decisions sit -- and where they have moved from. The honest version. Where you have ceded authority you used to hold, and where you have re-claimed authority that had drifted.
03
Signal Monitoring
The two or three observable signals you will track weekly -- the ones that would tell you the operating system is failing before the heatmap re-run does. Specific. Not "morale".
04
Intervention Triggers
What pulls you out of the cadence and into a structural intervention. Specific. The thing that, if it happens, means you re-run the heatmap before the calendar reminds you to.
05
Team Development Moves
The deliberate moves to grow the team into the AI-era roles. Not generic "career development". Specific moves with named people and dates.
06
The Leader's Own Protocol
What you will do as the leader to hold the operating system. Not a self-care line. Specific protocols -- writing rhythms, calibration check-ins, your own peer-to-peer cadence.
3
Step 3
Write the Lessons Synthesis

The Lessons Synthesis is the part of Capstone Part B that proves the journey worked -- not that you read the materials well. Write it in your own voice. Not the language of the course. What do you actually now know that you did not know in Week 1? Where did the audit, the blueprint, the org chart, the conversation, and the heatmap land in your thinking? Name at least one belief you came in with that turned out to be wrong and what replaced it. The voice should sound like you, on a Friday afternoon, talking to a peer who asked what the six weeks taught you.

LESSONS SYNTHESIS
In Your Own Voice
What You Now Know That You Did Not Know in Week 1

Cumulative thinking across the six Practicals. What pattern recognition is now automatic that was effortful before. What belief you came in with turned out to be wrong, and what replaced it. The Synthesis is short -- aim for three to four paragraphs of dense, particular, voice-driven writing. Not a course summary.

AI-Powered Activity

Use AI to Pressure-Test the Plan and the Synthesis

Capstone Part B fails at two predictable places -- the operating plan reads as aspirational rather than deployable, and the Lessons Synthesis collapses into the language of the course rather than the practitioner's own voice. AI as an outside reader catches both. Use it before you submit -- this is the half of the Capstone that determines whether the journey produced a leader or just a learner.

1
Stress-Test the Operating Plan -- Deployable vs Aspirational
Ask AI to test whether the six sections are deployable on Monday morning -- with cadences, decisions, signals, and triggers that someone other than you could pick up and run -- or whether they have slipped into aspirational language.
Paste this prompt
I am writing a 90-day operating plan as Capstone Part B for a course on leading AI-ready teams. The six sections are: (1) Meeting Cadence: [paste]. (2) Decision Rights: [paste]. (3) Signal Monitoring: [paste]. (4) Intervention Triggers: [paste]. (5) Team Development Moves: [paste]. (6) The Leader's Own Protocol: [paste]. Pressure-test on three criteria. (1) For each section, could a peer pick this up on Monday morning and tell you what they would actually do this week? (2) Which section is most aspirational -- closer to a description of a good leader than to a deployable instruction? (3) Where would the plan most likely fail in Week 7 -- and is the failure mode addressed in the plan itself, or assumed away? Tell me which section needs the most rewriting and rewrite the weakest one with operational specificity.
Which section was weakest? What is the rewritten version?
2
Test the Synthesis -- Pattern Recognition vs Course Recap
Ask AI to test whether the Lessons Synthesis is genuine pattern recognition in your own voice -- or whether it has slipped into the language of the course, listing the Practicals back rather than naming what you now actually know.
Paste this prompt
My Lessons Synthesis is: [paste]. Pressure-test on three criteria. (1) Does this read in my own voice -- specific to my team, my rollout, my pattern -- or has it slipped into generic course language ("I learned the importance of empathy", "I now value team development")? (2) Have I named at least one specific belief I came in with that turned out to be wrong, and what replaced it -- or have I only named what I added, which lets every prior belief stand? (3) Is the pattern recognition genuinely automatic now, or am I still describing the Practicals back rather than what they have left me with? Where the Synthesis is generic, rewrite the weakest paragraph with the specifics of my team and my rollout.
Where did the Synthesis go generic? What is the voice-driven version?

Take This to Peer-to-Peer

Before you submit, talk it through.

Capstone Part B falls apart at the same place every time -- the plan reads well on the page but no one could run it on Monday, and the Synthesis sounds like the course rather than the practitioner. A peer who has done their own Capstone can hear both faults at the same speed an assessor will.

What to bring

Read the six plan sections aloud. After each one, ask your peer one question: if you handed them this section and asked them to run it next week, would they know what to do? Then read the Synthesis. If their first reaction is "this sounds like you" -- the voice has held. If their first reaction is "this could have come from anyone in the cohort" -- it has slipped into course language and you have one more pass to make.

Three questions for your peer to ask you
  • Of the six plan sections, which one would you struggle to run on Monday morning even though you wrote it -- and what is missing from it that would make it deployable?
  • The Synthesis -- can you point to one belief you came in with that turned out to be wrong, and what replaced it? If not, you have only added, not changed.
  • If the second-generation AI rollout starts in Week 13, which section of the plan breaks first -- and is that breakage already named in the plan, or assumed away?
