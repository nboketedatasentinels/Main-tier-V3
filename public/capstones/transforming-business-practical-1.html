The Door Practice Log

Five real meetings this week. Before each one, run the Door Practice -- read the room before you walk in. AI fluency in the room. The unspoken board-level definition of success. The vendor narrative shaping executive perception. Then capture what shifted in your behaviour because you read the room first. At least one of the five must be a meeting where the AI fluency was asymmetric -- the room had more than you, or you had more than the room. The asymmetry direction is part of the data.

What good looks like
Aim for these as you go.
  • Five real meetings from this actual work week, not reverse-engineered from memory at the end.
  • Each entry names what you read about the room -- AI fluency, the unspoken board-level success definition, the vendor narrative shaping the conversation -- before you contributed.
  • At least one entry honestly names asymmetric AI fluency -- including the entry where you had less than the room, not just where you had more.
  • At least one entry references a vendor narrative (Microsoft AI, OpenAI, the Big Four advisor, the local systems integrator) that was shaping executive perception in the room.
  • Each entry names what shifted in your behaviour -- the question you did not ask, the framing you swapped, the silence you held longer -- because you ran the Door Practice first.

Step 3
Name the Pattern Across the Five

Five entries are data, not a Practical. The Practical is what the five together reveal that no single entry would. Two checks. First: across all five, what was the most consistent thing you read about the room that you had not been reading before? Second: where in your week did the Door Practice not happen this time, and what was the cost? Naming the pattern is what makes this Practical generalise. Without it, you have done five entries, not changed an instinct.

01
The Pattern
Across the five entries -- what was the most consistent piece of data you were reading about rooms that you had not been reading before? One sentence.
02
Where the Practice Did Not Happen
The meeting this week where you did not run the Door Practice -- and the cost of skipping it. Honest, not heroic.
AI-Powered Activity

Use AI to Stress-Test the Log

Door Practice logs fail in two predictable places -- the practitioner reverse-engineers entries from memory at the end of the week (so the data is reconstructed not captured), and the asymmetric-fluency entry only ever names the direction where the practitioner had more. Both make the log unmarkable and unrepeatable. AI as an outside reader catches both. Use it before you submit.

1
Test Whether the Five Were Logged in Real Time
Ask AI to test whether each entry reads like it was captured the day of the meeting -- or like it was reconstructed on Friday afternoon when the Practical was due.
Paste this prompt
I am submitting a five-day Door Practice Log. The five entries are: [paste]. Pressure-test this. Specifically: (1) Does each entry contain detail that could only have been captured close to the meeting -- a specific time, a specific phrase, a specific moment of silence -- or have the entries been smoothed into a coherent narrative arc that reads like Friday-afternoon reconstruction? (2) Are the counterfactuals ('what would have happened without the Door Practice') different in shape across the five, or do they sound like the same template applied five times? (3) Which entry sounds least like the actual day it claims to describe? Tell me which entry is at risk of having been reverse-engineered and what would make it more credible.
Which entry is at risk and what would make it more credible?
2
Test the Asymmetric-Fluency Honesty
Ask AI to test whether the asymmetric-fluency entries are honest about the direction -- including the entries where the practitioner had less than the room, not only where they had more.
Paste this prompt
In my five Door Practice entries, I have flagged the AI fluency in each room as symmetric, asymmetric-up (I had more), or asymmetric-down (the room had more). My five reads are: [paste]. Pressure-test the honesty. (1) Across the five entries, how many times did I claim asymmetric-up versus asymmetric-down? (2) If I claimed asymmetric-up four times out of five, that is a flag -- a senior practitioner walking into five different rooms and being the most fluent in four of them is unlikely to be reading the rooms accurately. (3) Push me on the entries where I claimed symmetric or asymmetric-up -- is there evidence in the entry itself that I was actually less fluent than the room on a specific dimension (model governance, vendor commercials, regulator detail) and I am defending against admitting it?
Where might the asymmetric-fluency reads be defensive rather than honest?
AI Lens
Three months from now, when you have run the Door Practice on twenty more meetings, what is the read that you will have started to make automatically -- the one that you currently have to remind yourself to look for? And what is the read you are still ducking, even with the discipline?
Take This to Peer-to-Peer

Before you submit, talk it through.

Door Practice logs fall apart at the same place every time -- the practitioner reconstructs the entries on Friday afternoon, and the asymmetric-fluency entries only ever name the direction where the practitioner had more. A peer who has logged their own week can hear both at the same speed an assessor will. Bring this to your peer-to-peer call this week.

What to bring

Read out the entry you trust the least first -- the one where the data feels thinnest, or where you wrote the entry late. If your peer's first reaction is 'that sounds like it was written on Friday afternoon', the log is at risk and the rest is too. Then read the entry where you flagged the fluency asymmetry. If the direction was up, your peer should be able to ask one credible question that would force the entry to admit a downward asymmetry on a specific dimension -- and you should be able to hear it without defending.

Three questions for your peer to ask you
  • The entry you wrote the latest -- does it have a specific moment, phrase, or time that anchors it to the actual day, or is it a smoothed narrative reconstruction?
  • Across your five fluency reads -- is there an entry where you claimed asymmetric-up that should have been asymmetric-down on a specific dimension you are not yet comfortable naming?
  • The pattern you named across the five -- if you ran another week of Door Practice tomorrow, what would the pattern predict about Tuesday morning? If it cannot predict anything, the pattern is post-hoc not real.
