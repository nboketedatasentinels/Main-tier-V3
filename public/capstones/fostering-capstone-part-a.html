The Heart-Centered Leader Blueprint

Your personal operating model for leading an AI-era team. Five sections that integrate the EQ pillar you are strengthening, the alternative behaviour you are practising, the recurring moments where the old behaviour ran you, what empathy looks like for you in those moments, and the one 30-day metric you will track. The Blueprint should function as a daily reference -- something you could pin to your desk and use to run yourself.
Outcomes assessed: LO1 (Analyse leadership presence and team dynamics), LO2 (Design and demonstrate a heart-centred leadership blueprint)
Word count: 600-800 words total across all sections.
Capstone Part A of 2 · Combined Capstone = 50% of competence pass
What good looks like
A Blueprint that functions as a daily operating model -- specific, observable, and runnable starting Monday morning.
  • The EQ pillar named is the genuinely lowest one (often uncomfortable to admit), not the safest one to write about.
  • The alternative behaviour is operationally specific -- a move a colleague could observe -- not "communicate better" or "be more empathetic".
  • The three recurring moments are real situations from your AI-era team work, not hypothetical patterns.
  • Empathy in Section 4 is described at the level of behaviour, not feeling -- what you would say or do, not what you would feel.
  • The 30-day metric in Section 5 is observable to a manager -- a behaviour change someone else could see -- not internal tracking only.
  • The Blueprint as a whole could be pinned to a desk and used as a daily operating reference, not read once and filed away.

Section 01
Outcome 1 · Pillar identification

The EQ pillar I am strengthening

Of the five pillars of heart-centred leadership -- self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skill -- name the one you are strengthening through this Blueprint. State why this pillar specifically, and what it has been costing your AI-era team work in concrete terms (a decision delayed, a person disengaged, a meeting derailed -- not "team morale").
The pillar is named with the framework. The naming should pass the test of being uncomfortable to admit -- if it feels safe, it is probably the wrong one. The cost has to be concrete and tied specifically to AI-era work (rollout pressure, role redesign, technical authority shift, change fatigue compounding), not generic team friction.

Section 02
Outcome 2 · Alternative behaviour

The behaviour I am practising instead of my default

Name your default behaviour in the moments that involve the pillar you are strengthening. Then name the alternative behaviour you are practising instead. The alternative needs to be operationally specific -- something a colleague could see you do -- not aspirational language.
The default is named honestly (the test: would a peer who works with you recognise it?). The alternative is a specific move with a specific trigger -- something that activates in a specific kind of moment, not 'I will be more open'. Two operational tests: a colleague can see you do it; you could practise it starting Monday morning without preparation.
Suggested length: 100-140 words 0 words
Section 03
Outcome 1 + 2 · Recurring moments

Three recurring moments where the old behaviour used to run me

Name three specific recurring moments from your AI-era team work where the old behaviour used to run you. Each moment should be specific -- a real situation type, with the trigger and what tended to happen. These are the moments where the alternative behaviour from Section 2 will show up.
Three real situations from your work, not three categories. Each one names the trigger (what kicks the default behaviour off), what tended to happen, and what was at stake when the old behaviour ran. The moments should be different enough that the alternative behaviour gets tested across different conditions -- not three versions of the same situation.
Suggested length: 180-240 words 0 words
Section 04
Outcome 1 + 2 · Empathy in those moments

What empathy looks like for me in those moments

For each of the three moments in Section 3, describe what empathy looks like in action -- what you would say, what you would do, what you would not do. The description should pass the test of being observable: a colleague watching you in that moment could see whether you ran the empathic move or fell back to the default.
Empathy named at the level of behaviour, not feeling. The test is whether your empathy could be observed by someone else -- 'I would feel more compassionate' is not a behaviour; 'I would ask the question and then stay silent for ten seconds' is. Different moments may call for different empathic moves; collapsing them into a single generic posture misses the discipline.

Section 05
Outcome 2 · 30-day metric

The one metric I will track for 30 days

Name the one metric you will track for 30 days to know whether the alternative behaviour is producing the change you want. The metric must be observable to someone other than you -- a behaviour your team or your manager could see -- not internal feeling. Specify the cadence (when you check it) and what would tell you the alternative is working versus what would tell you it is not.
Observable means another person could verify it without asking you how you feel. Examples of the level of specificity required: 'how many times this week a senior engineer brought a problem and I asked a question rather than answering' or 'how many of the 1:1s this week ended with the team member talking more than I did'. Internal feeling metrics do not meet the bar.

Take This to Peer-to-Peer

Before you submit, walk a peer through the Blueprint.

The Blueprint is meant to be a daily operating model. The fastest test is whether someone who knows your work can read it and predict what you would do in a real situation tomorrow. If the peer cannot, the Blueprint is journaling rather than operating.

Questions for your peer to ask you
  • Is the pillar in Section 1 the genuinely lowest one, or the safest one to write about? What would make you uncomfortable to admit?
  • In Section 2, could a colleague see you run the alternative behaviour, or would they only see the result? If they could not see it, is it operational?
  • Are the three moments in Section 3 different enough that the alternative behaviour gets tested across different conditions, or are they three versions of the same situation?
  • Is the metric in Section 5 observable to someone other than you, or is it tracking your internal feeling?
