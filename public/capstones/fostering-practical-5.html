Heatmap Before the Exit Interview

Score the team across six dimensions against observable behaviour in the past 30 days. Then name the highest-risk role -- the specific person, not a role family. Name the early signal you have been ignoring. And design the intervention you will run -- with a date, named participants, and a 30-day success measure that addresses the cause, not the surface signal.

What good looks like
Aim for these as you go.
  • Each dimension scored against observable behaviour in the past 30 days, not gut feel.
  • Highest-risk role named with specificity -- a person by role and ideally name -- including why this person specifically.
  • The ignored signal is one you have actually been ignoring -- not one you have already started acting on.
  • The intervention addresses the cause, not the surface signal. No one-off coffees pretending to be interventions.
  • The intervention has a date, named participants, and a 30-day success measure that is observable.
1
Step 1
Ground the Heatmap in the Past 30 Days

A heatmap is only useful if it is scored against what actually happened, not against the version of the team you are managing in your head. Before you score, name three to five real moments from the past 30 days that you will use as evidence. Specific meetings. Specific comments. Specific silences.

01
The Team
Name the team you are scoring -- size, function, your relationship to it. Two sentences.
02
The Evidence Window
Three to five real moments from the past 30 days you will score against. Specific. Datable.
2
Step 2
Score the Six Dimensions

For each dimension, score against the evidence window from Step 1. Green if it is observably healthy in the past 30 days. Amber if there is drift you can name but it is not yet acute. Red if there is a specific signal in the past 30 days that the team is at risk on this dimension. Then write what you are scoring against.

Work Meaning

Manager Relationship
The relationship between you and each person on the team is current. People are bringing real things to you. The 1:1s are not deteriorating into status updates because the work is being absorbed by AI tooling.

03
Growth Visibility
People can see what they are growing into in 12 months -- specifically in a world where AI tooling is changing what mastery looks like. The growth path is not "do what you do, but with the AI". It is named, visible, and credible.
Rating
04
Workload Sustainability
The team's pace is sustainable into Q3 and beyond. Change fatigue from compounding AI rollouts is being acknowledged in the workload, not just absorbed. The team is not running on overtime to compensate for the rollout's friction.
Rating
05
AI-Tooling Confidence
The team's confidence with the AI tooling is rising, not falling. People feel able to override the AI when their judgement says so. Pushback in technical reviews is being read as engineering, not as resistance.
Rating
06
Succession Risk
If the highest-context senior on the team left in 30 days, the team would still hold. Knowledge is not single-threaded through one person whose role is being most affected by the AI rollout. Cost of replacement is honest, not hopeful.

Step 3
Name the Role, the Ignored Signal, and the Intervention

Look at the heatmap. The highest-risk role is the specific person whose situation lights up across multiple dimensions -- and who you would most regret losing in 90 days. Not a role family. A person. Then name the early signal you have been ignoring on them. The honest one -- not the one you have already started acting on. Then design the intervention. Cause, not surface.

01
Highest-Risk Role
Role and ideally name (or initial). One sentence on why this person specifically.
02
The Ignored Signal
The signal you have actually been ignoring -- not one you have already started acting on.
The Intervention
What will you actually run -- and how will you know in 30 days whether it worked?

Address the cause, not the surface signal. A one-off coffee is not an intervention. A generic "I'll check in more" is not an intervention. The cause here is what is making the signal show up -- usually structural, not personal.

Structural change to how disagreement is heard Re-establishment of expert authority in the room Workload or scope reset Named role redesign conversation
AI-Powered Activity

Use AI to Pressure-Test the Heatmap

Heatmaps fail at the same two places every time -- the practitioner names a signal they have already started acting on (which lets them off the hook), and the intervention is a surface fix dressed up as a structural one. AI as an outside reader can hear both faults faster than a peer can. Use it before you take this Practical to peer-to-peer.

1
Test Whether the Ignored Signal Is Genuinely Ignored
Ask AI to test whether the signal you have named is one you have actually been ignoring -- or one you have already started acting on, named here to feel like the work has been done.
Paste this prompt
I am running a Heatmap before the exit interview on my team. The highest-risk role is: [paste your highest-risk role]. The signal I have named as the early signal I have been ignoring is: [paste your ignored signal]. Pressure-test this. Specifically: (1) Is this a signal I have actually been ignoring, or one I have already started acting on -- and named here because it is comfortable to admit? (2) What is the harder signal I might be ignoring that this one is sitting in front of? (3) If a peer who knows this person independently scored the situation, what would they name as the signal that is actually being missed?
What did AI flag? What is the harder signal you have actually been ignoring?
2
Stress-Test the Intervention -- Cause vs Surface Fix
Ask AI to test whether the intervention addresses the cause or whether it is a surface fix -- a one-off coffee, a generic check-in, a vague "I will be more present" -- dressed up to look structural.
Paste this prompt
My intervention for the highest-risk role is: [paste your intervention name and shape]. The cause I have named is: [paste the cause]. The 30-day success measure is: [paste the success measure]. Pressure-test on three criteria. (1) Does the intervention actually address the cause I have named, or does it address a different (easier) version of the cause? (2) Is the success measure observable -- a specific behaviour change in 30 days -- or is it vague ("feel more engaged", "be back in the conversation")? (3) If the intervention runs as designed and the success measure is met, would the underlying dynamic actually have shifted -- or would I have just papered over the signal? Tell me where the intervention is closer to a surface fix than a structural one, and rewrite the weakest part.
Where did the intervention go surface? What is the structural version?
AI Lens
Six months from now, when the AI rollout has shifted again -- new tooling, new role definitions, a new pressure cycle -- which of the six heatmap dimensions is most likely to drop a rating, and what observable signal would tell you to re-run the heatmap before the calendar reminds you to?
Take This to Peer-to-Peer

Before you submit, talk it through.

Heatmaps fall apart at the same two places every time -- the ignored signal is one the practitioner has already started acting on, and the intervention is a one-off coffee with a different label on it. A peer who has done their own heatmap can hear both at the same speed an assessor will. Bring this Practical to your peer-to-peer call this week.

What to bring

Read the heatmap aloud, in order. Then read the highest-risk role, the ignored signal, and the intervention. If your peer's first reaction at the intervention is "could you do that on Tuesday?" -- the intervention is structural. If their first reaction is "what does that actually mean in practice?" -- it is still a surface fix wearing a structural label.

Three questions for your peer to ask you
  • Is the highest-risk role named with a person and a specific reason -- or is it a role family wearing a name?
  • The ignored signal -- did you name the one you have actually been ignoring, or the one you have already started acting on because that one is comfortable to admit?
  • If you ran the intervention exactly as designed and hit the 30-day measure, would the underlying dynamic have shifted -- or would you have just papered over the signal?
