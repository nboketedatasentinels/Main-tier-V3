The 90-Day Operating Plan

Your operating system for leading the AI-era team you have today, not the one you wish you had. Six sections that integrate the team's current state on the five pillars and the Real Org Chart, the Heatmap reading, the conversations to be had over 30 / 60 / 90 days, the system reset moves, your leader's operating cadence, and the success indicators -- plus the AI Integration section drawing on one Digital Edge exercise. The Plan should be defensible to a CEO or CHRO without further explanation.
Outcomes assessed: LO1 (Analyse patterns), LO3 (Distinguish formal from real org chart; lead through it), LO4 (Construct and execute the difficult conversation), LO5 (Evaluate early signals; design interventions), LO6 (Integrate AI tools with judgement)
Word count: 800-1000 words total across all sections.
Capstone Part B of 2 · Combined Capstone = 50% of competence pass
What good looks like
A Plan that reads as a connected operating system -- each section drawing on the prior weeks of the Journey, defensible to senior stakeholders without your personal explanation.
  • The five-pillar reading and Real Org Chart in Section 1 reflect honest assessment of the team you actually have, including the uncomfortable parts.
  • The Heatmap in Section 2 names the highest-risk role specifically -- not "the team is at risk" -- with the early signal you have been ignoring named honestly.
  • The 30 / 60 / 90 day conversations in Section 3 are connected to the Real Org Chart and the Heatmap -- they are not free-standing.
  • The system reset moves in Section 4 address cause, not symptom -- structural changes, not motivational gestures.
  • The leader's operating cadence in Section 5 is sustainable beyond 90 days -- specific recurring rhythms, calendar slots, accountability mechanisms.
  • The success indicators in Section 6 are observable -- a manager-level external rater could see whether the indicator has shifted in 90 days.
  • The AI Integration in Section 7 demonstrates evaluative judgement -- what was kept, what was rejected, why -- not uncritical adoption.
  • The Plan as a whole is defensible to a CEO or CHRO who was not in the Journey, with no need for personal explanation.

Section 03
Outcome 4 · Conversations 30 / 60 / 90

Conversations to be had in the next 30 / 60 / 90 days

Name the specific conversations you will have at the 30 / 60 / 90 day marks. For each conversation: who (by role), what the conversation is about, what you have to be willing to hear, and how it connects to the Real Org Chart from Section 1 or the Heatmap from Section 2. The conversations are not free-standing.
Each conversation connects back to a specific element of Section 1 or Section 2. A conversation that does not is either out of scope for this Plan or signals a gap in the Plan's coherence. 'What you have to be willing to hear' is the discipline -- naming what would make you defensive, and committing to staying open to it. The 30 / 60 / 90 cadence sequences the conversations in a way that makes operational sense for the team's situation.

Section 04
Outcome 5 · System reset moves

System reset moves -- structural, not motivational

Name two or three system reset moves that address the cause of what Section 1 and Section 2 surfaced -- not the symptom. The moves are structural (a recurring meeting redesigned, an escalation pathway changed, a decision-rights reallocation, a feedback loop created), not motivational gestures. For each move: what changes, who is responsible, by when.
Structural means the move changes how the system runs, not how people feel about it. The test: if you put the move in place and then disappeared for two weeks, would the change still be operating? If yes, it is structural. If no, it depends on your presence and is motivational. Each move addresses a specific cause from Section 1 or Section 2 -- not a generic team improvement.

Section 05
Outcome 5 · Leader's operating cadence

The leader's operating cadence -- sustainable beyond 90 days

Name your operating cadence -- the recurring rhythms, calendar slots, and accountability mechanisms that hold this Plan together. The cadence should be runnable beyond the 90 days without your willpower carrying it. Specify what happens daily, weekly, and monthly, and what mechanism keeps you accountable to it.
Sustainable means built on calendar slots, not on motivation. The test: if you had a stressful month, would the cadence survive? Anything that depends on you remembering to do it does not. Name the accountability mechanism specifically -- a peer, a partner, a recurring check-in, a public commitment -- not 'I will hold myself accountable'.

Section 06
Outcome 5 · Success indicators

Success indicators -- observable in 90 days

Name the success indicators that will tell you, at 90 days, whether this Plan is producing the change you want. The indicators should be observable to someone other than you -- a behaviour change in a named person, a meeting dynamic change, a metric a manager could verify. Specify what would tell you to escalate the Plan and what would tell you to reset it.
Observable means a manager-level external rater could see whether the indicator has shifted, without you explaining it. Internal feeling indicators do not meet the bar. The escalate/reset triggers protect the Plan from drifting -- without them, ambiguous results lead to a gradually-weakening Plan.

Section 07
Outcome 6 · AI Integration

AI Integration -- judgement, not adoption

Select one Digital Edge exercise output from this Module (the RACI conversation script, the Real Org Chart prompt, the Heatmap prompt, or another). Name what you kept from the AI output, what you rejected, and why -- including any instance where you overrode the AI suggestion with your own judgement. Demonstrate evaluative judgement, not uncritical adoption.
The discipline is evaluative -- not 'AI gave me good ideas'. Name a specific output, name what you kept and why, name what you rejected and why. If everything was kept, you have summarised, not evaluated. The override case is the strongest evidence: a moment where the AI suggested one move and you chose another, with the reasoning named.

Take This to Peer-to-Peer

Before you submit, run the Plan past a peer.

The 90-Day Operating Plan is meant to be defensible to a CEO or CHRO without your personal explanation. The fastest test is whether a peer can read it and predict what you would do in the next 30 days. If the peer cannot, the Plan is intent rather than operating.

Questions for your peer to ask you
  • Could a CEO or CHRO read this Plan and act on it without asking you for additional context?
  • Do the conversations in Section 3 connect to the Real Org Chart and Heatmap, or are they free-standing?
  • Are the system reset moves in Section 4 structural, or do they depend on your willpower or presence?
  • In Section 7 -- did you actually evaluate the AI output, or did you summarise what it said? Where did you override it?
