The case · read first
MTN Nigeria and MTN South Africa, 2023-2024
Collapse
Background

MTN Group is Africa's largest mobile network operator, headquartered in Johannesburg, South Africa, with major operations in Nigeria, Ghana, Uganda, Cameroon, and 13 other markets. Across 2022 and 2023, MTN published its 'Ambition 2025' strategy emphasising AI-driven network operations as a core capability investment. Public statements from CEO Ralph Mupita and Group CTIO Mazen Mroue across 2023 and 2024 referenced AI-driven network optimisation, predictive maintenance, and automated fault detection as strategic priorities.

Within MTN Nigeria and MTN South Africa specifically, AI-assisted network operations tools were rolled out across 2023 and 2024 to support engineering teams. The tools were positioned as augmentation -- supporting human engineers rather than replacing them. Senior network engineers who held deep knowledge of specific network segments -- some with 12-15 years of context on regional infrastructure quirks -- were expected to validate and supervise AI-generated recommendations.

By mid-2024, a pattern had emerged across both operations. Senior network engineers were increasingly accepting AI recommendations without challenge -- not because the recommendations were always right, but because the social and political cost of pushing back had risen. Multiple practitioners reported in industry publications and at the AfricaCom 2024 conference (held in Cape Town) that informal escalation paths had narrowed, that 'I disagree with the AI' was being read by middle management as resistance to transformation rather than informed engineering judgement, and that the senior engineers who had previously been the institutional memory of regional networks had stopped asking the awkward questions in technical reviews.

Public reporting by African Business and TechCabal across 2024 and early 2025 documented voluntary attrition rising in MTN's senior network engineering function in both Nigeria and South Africa. MTN's published group annual reports (2023 and 2024) acknowledged 'changes in engineering team composition' as part of the digital transformation programme, framed as positive workforce evolution. Senior engineers who left have spoken publicly on LinkedIn about the dynamic of being unable to disagree productively with AI-generated recommendations within the existing team culture.

The dynamic to analyse

This case is not a story about whether the AI tools were technically capable. It is a story about how the formal org chart and the real delivery organisation diverged once the AI tools were introduced -- and what happened to the senior engineers whose informal expert power was suddenly competing with AI-generated outputs in technical review meetings.

The questions below ask you to map the divergence between formal and real authority, identify the conversation that was not happening, and design the early-signal intervention that should have been run before the senior engineers started leaving.

Question 02
Outcome 5 · Early signal

Identify the early signal -- and why it was misread

Identify the early signal of disengagement that was visible months before the senior engineers started leaving. Why was this signal misread inside MTN, and what would a Heatmap reading have surfaced that the formal performance metrics did not?
The early signal lives at the level of behaviour, not sentiment -- something a manager could have observed in a meeting, not something they had to infer from a survey. The misreading explanation works structurally: not "the manager was bad" but "the existing reading frame made the misreading the easier conclusion". The Heatmap dimensions you name should be ones that the formal metrics could not have captured.

Question 03
Outcome 4 · The conversation

Construct the conversation an MTN engineering leader should have had

Construct the conversation that an MTN engineering leader should have had with one of the senior engineers in mid-2024. Provide the opening line verbatim, name what the leader would need to be willing to hear, and describe what would need to shift in the conversation for the senior engineer to feel safe asking the awkward questions again.
A verbatim opening line, not a summary of what the leader would say. What the leader has to be willing to hear is the discipline -- a one-off conversation that does not change how disagreement is handled in technical review will not produce a sustained shift. A structural shift is the point; a more empathetic tone is not.

Question 04
Outcomes 3 + 4 + 5 · Application to your own scope

Apply the same discipline to a senior expert in your own scope

Identify a senior expert in your own organisational scope who is at risk of becoming the person who stopped asking. Name them by role, name the AI tooling dynamic that is creating the risk, and name the specific conversation you will book with them in the next 14 days.
This is the highest-weighted question. The expert needs to be specific (by role; their actual situation, not a hypothetical). The AI tooling dynamic is concrete -- a specific tool, a specific kind of recommendation, a specific kind of meeting where the expert's pushback used to count. The conversation booked is in your calendar in the next 14 days, not 'I will reach out'.
