The case · read first
Klarna, February 2024 -- and the May 2025 reversal
Collapse
Background

In February 2024, Klarna -- the Swedish buy-now-pay-later fintech founded in 2005 by Sebastian Siemiatkowski and headquartered in Stockholm -- publicly announced that an AI assistant built on OpenAI technology was performing the work equivalent of 700 full-time customer service agents. The AI handled two-thirds of customer service chats in its first month, was available in 23 markets and 35 languages, resolved cases in under two minutes versus the previous 11-minute average, and was projected to drive an estimated 40 million US dollars in profit improvement in 2024.

The announcement was framed as a transformation case study. Klarna had reduced its workforce from approximately 5,000 to roughly 3,500 employees over the previous 18 months. Siemiatkowski publicly attributed a meaningful portion of the reduction to AI capability and signalled that more roles would be displaced. The narrative was widely covered in Bloomberg, the Financial Times, the Wall Street Journal, and Reuters.

In May 2025 -- 15 months later -- Siemiatkowski reversed course publicly. In an interview with Bloomberg in May 2025, he acknowledged that customer service quality had degraded, that customers were complaining about the AI's inability to handle complex or emotional cases, and that Klarna would be re-hiring human customer service agents. He stated that:

"Investing in human, human-quality service is a critical investment for the future of the brand."

The company began a deliberate rebuild of its human customer service capability while keeping AI in a supporting rather than substitutive role.

Internally during the same period, Klarna's customer service leadership team had been raising concerns through 2024 about training, dignity in the workplace, and team morale. Multiple LinkedIn posts from former Klarna customer service leaders in late 2024 and early 2025 described a team that had stopped asking questions, that had moved from a culture of customer empathy to one of metric-chasing, and that had quietly begun looking elsewhere. The senior leaders who had built the previous customer service culture left during 2024 and early 2025.

The dynamic to analyse

This case is not primarily a story about whether AI can do customer service work. It is a story about a senior practitioner who introduced a transformation that exposed which pillar of his leadership had been holding the team together -- and which pillar had not. The reversal was not driven by AI capability changing. It was driven by the human dynamics of the team and the customer base catching up with what had been put in place.

The questions below ask you to identify the leadership pillar that cracked first, why the rollout exposed it specifically, and what an AI-ready leadership blueprint would have looked like in early 2024.

Question 01
Outcome 1 · Pillar identification

Which pillar cracked first inside Klarna's customer service organisation?

Of the five pillars of heart-centred leadership -- self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skill -- which one cracked first inside Klarna's customer service organisation during 2024? Justify your answer in 2-3 sentences using evidence from the case.
Pick one pillar and defend it with case evidence. The justification has to point to specific elements in the case that fit the pillar you chose -- the senior leader exits, the team that stopped asking, the customer service quality degradation, the metric-chasing culture. The safest pillar to pick is rarely the one the case actually shows under stress.

Question 02
Outcome 1 · Pattern beyond the rollout

What does the senior leader exit pattern reveal beyond the AI rollout itself?

The senior customer service leaders who left Klarna in 2024 and early 2025 had been raising concerns through 2024. What does this pattern -- senior practitioners raising concerns and then leaving -- reveal about the team dynamic, beyond the AI rollout itself?
The test is whether you treat the AI rollout as cause or as exposure. A culture pattern that pre-existed the rollout would have surfaced under any sufficient pressure -- the rollout did not invent it. The argument has to use the case as evidence: the timing of the concerns, the timing of the exits, what had to be true for senior practitioners to raise concerns and then leave rather than escalate.

Question 03
Outcome 2 · Blueprint design

Design the alternative behaviour for Siemiatkowski

If you had been advising Siemiatkowski in early 2024 on a heart-centred leadership blueprint for the AI rollout, what specific behaviour would you have asked him to practise instead of the default he ran? Name the recurring moment, the alternative behaviour, and the observable metric you would have asked him to track for 90 days.
Three things to name -- the recurring moment (a specific moment in his leadership where the default behaviour ran), the alternative behaviour (operationally specific, not "communicate better"), and an observable 90-day metric (something a manager could see, not internal feeling). The test is whether the blueprint could be run starting Monday morning by Siemiatkowski himself.
